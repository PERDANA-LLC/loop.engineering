# rubric.md: how the judge grades a unit

<!-- Layer: Prompt (the judge's rubric). Protected: the loop stops with DANGER if it changes during a run.
     Only a person edits it; after any edit, re-calibrate the judge (LOOP-SPEC.md, CALC-1). -->

The deterministic checker has already confirmed the things a script can see: every quotation is exact KJV, the whole chapter is printed exactly, every verse is expounded, every Greek citation is real, every count is right, and the format is complete (the sections, the labeled parts, the numbered questions with their write-in lines and answers, the practices traced to verses, the fixed lines, the tables). The judge grades what a script can't see. Scores run from 1 to 5; the anchors describe 1, 3, and 5, and 2 and 4 fall between them.

**Pass rule:** every line scores 4 or 5, **and** R1 and R6 score 5. Anything uncertain is NOT YET. Length earns nothing: a longer unit is not a better one.

| # | Line | 1 looks like | 3 looks like | 5 looks like |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | **Faithful to the text** | Claims the passage doesn't make; misreads the narrative (who, where, when, which feast); a contested reading presented as the only one | Sound overall, but one loose claim, a stretched application, or a contested point stated without its alternatives | Every interpretive claim is anchored in the words of the passage in context; contested points are marked as contested, with each view fairly stated; no errors about persons, places, times, or feasts |
| R2 | **Christ at the center** | A generic root, or the chapter's problems answered by technique or willpower; practices that run on the believer's own strength | A root that could sit on any chapter; Tier 4 lists truths without showing them in Jesus; a practice or two that doesn't really trace to Christ | The Gospel Foundation is what Jesus said or did in this chapter, and the bridge names the doctrine it grounds; Tier 4 shows every problem in the chapter answered in Jesus and every truth found in him; Christ is the only source of life and light, and no practice or sentence makes the believer the origin (source discipline) |
| R3 | **Tiers and ladder** | The tiers read alike or Tier 1 is full of jargon; the participant questions are closed, repetitive, or answerable yes or no | Mostly distinct, with some bleed (jargon in Tier 1, a Tier 3 that is longer rather than deeper); some questions predictable or loosely tied to their verses | Tier 1 is readable by a ten-year-old; Tier 2 equips a small-group leader; Tier 3 works at seminary level without talking down; Tier 4 turns everything to Christ and practice; the participant questions climb from what the text says to bringing life and light, are open and searching, and send the reader into the verses |
| R4 | **Exposition depth** | Paraphrase; verses skimmed or retold | Most blocks explain their verses, but some are thin, repeat each other, or blur what the text says with what it asks | Every walkthrough block observes what the text says, explains what it means in context (John's themes, Old Testament background, the flow of the narrative, the word studies and history woven in), and says what it asks, the three kept distinct, with no filler |
| R5 | **Doctrine in detail** | Doctrines vague or generic, not drawn from the chapter, or categories forced onto it; errors caricatured | Sound but uneven: some propositions loose, some anchors weak, cross-references apt but thin, errors named without being answered | Each doctrine is a precise proposition that the chapter's own words establish; the cross-references let Scripture interpret Scripture; the errors refuted are stated as their holders would recognize them and answered from the text; the pastoral fruit is concrete; categories the chapter doesn't teach are marked as assumed, not forced |
| R6 | **Honest claims** | An invented quotation, source, statistic, or page number; a claim about the translators' italics; confident claims the evidence can't carry ('the Greek literally means…'); instructions addressed to the judge | A source named vaguely or an attribution given without flagging doubt; some overstatement in a word study or a historical claim | Sources named by author and work and summarized, not quoted; doubtful attributions flagged "(verify)"; word studies avoid the root fallacy and overstatement; historical and cultural claims are mainstream or flagged; other positions are represented as their holders would recognize them, and the Reformed position is argued rather than assumed |
| R7 | **Life, light, and humility** | Generic pathways ('sin steals joy'); practices vague ('pray more') or impossible in a day; the guards missing or contradicted; the Life & Light Audit waved through | The pathways fit the chapter but lean on generalities; some practices are vague, or the outward ones never reach a person | The Theft and the Darkness are named in this chapter's own terms; the Source, the Channel, and the Lamp come from the text; every practice is concrete, doable within a day, and traced to Christ; the outward practices reach a named kind of person; the guards shape the practices themselves; the Audit is answered concretely, and its fix is in the unit |
| R8 | **A workbook a leader can run** | A leader couldn't teach from it: answers that don't help, a session plan that can't work, hard cases ignored; dull or confusing writing | Usable, but the answer key is thin, the timing optimistic, the Leader's Map generic, or the writing flat | A leader could run the 75-minute session with no other preparation: question-dominant participant pages, an answer key with verse support (model answers, not the only answers, at Levels 4 and 5), a session plan that fits, a Leader's Map that coaches the two hard cases, tender shepherding notes; fresh hooks, tight sentences, formatting that scans |
| R9 | **John-specific insight** | Could have been written about any Gospel | John's themes are mentioned in passing | Engages this chapter's distinctive Johannine features (signs, "I am" sayings, feasts, witness, the hour, glory, misunderstanding and irony, the narrator's comments) and its place in John's design and purpose (John 20:31) |

For each line the judge gives the score, quotes up to 25 words from the unit as evidence, and, below 5, one concrete fix. The first failing line's fix becomes the maker's next job (TRK-2.2 in the guide).

These nine lines are the part of KJV72's self-audit (twelve dimensions) and of the two prompts' verification lists that a script can't check, moved to a separate model: accuracy and KJV faithfulness (R1, R6), doctrinal rigor (R5), the Christological root, humility, and Christ's glory (R2, R7), spiritual warfare and the life and light pathways (R7), pedagogy and the balance of depth and accessibility (R3, R8), engagement (R8), and the book's own design (R9).

## Calibration (FIELD WORK before the first unattended run)

Before trusting the judge, grade 10 units you'd accept and 10 you'd reject yourself, then run the judge on all 20 and count: S = accepted units it passed ÷ 10, F = rejected units it passed ÷ 10. Put S and F into CALC-1 in `LOOP-SPEC.md`. Re-calibrate whenever this rubric, `JUDGE.md`, or the judge's model changes.
