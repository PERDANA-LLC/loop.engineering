# Prompt upgrade proposals

KJV72's Spiral (bs3's Step C before it), made safe (EX-6.1 in the guide): the maker may add ideas here for improving `PROMPT.md`, `TEMPLATE.md`, `plan.md`, or `rubric.md`. It never edits those files itself. A person reads these, tries any change on a fixed set of units first, and records the decision in `LOOP-SPEC.md`'s changelog.

Format: `- <date> · john-NN · <file>: <the change, and why>`

- 2026-09-24 · john-02 · TEMPLATE.md: in Part 1 rule 3 or Part 4, say that a whole-verse quotation of a cross-reference inside a Key Verses section counts toward that section's whole-verse limit (the checker counts every full quote in the span), so cross-references there are best given as bare references or fragments. Also warn that a number after a reference and a comma is read as a follow-on verse ('John 2:20, 46 years' is checked as John 2:46 and fails). Both are easy to trip over and neither is stated.
  - **Decision (2026-09-24, the builder, before v1.0 shipped):** adopted both. TEMPLATE.md Part 4 now says whole-verse cross-references count toward a Key Verses limit; TEMPLATE.md rule 3 warns against a numeral after a reference and a comma; and the checker no longer reads a number followed by an ordinary word ('46 years') as a verse. See LOOP-SPEC.md, changelog v1.0 (SF-5).
- 2026-09-24 · john-02 · TEMPLATE.md / PROMPT.md: the v2.0 combined format runs long. A full-depth first draft of John 2 came to 20,602 words, over the 20,000 ceiling, and trimming it took three checker runs. Suggest that TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rule 8 give a working target (for example 14,000–17,000 words, with rough shares: Walkthrough ~4,500, Doctrine ~2,500, Leader's Guide ~3,500), and that the checker's LENGTH message list the five longest sections. Also suggest PROMPT.md say whether checker runs on a partly written file count toward the three-run limit, since the prompt itself recommends writing in parts.
  - **Decision (2026-09-24, the owner):** not adopted as a target: there is no word limit at all now (plan.md D10; LOOP-SPEC.md, SF-12).
- 2026-09-24 · john-02 · tools/lexicon.py data (for a person; not a PROMPT/TEMPLATE file): the G2411 entry prints `ἱερόν  translit: hieros`, the adjective's spelling (G2413 is also `hieros`). Because the checker takes the lexicon's translit as the lemma key, *hieron* (G2411, …) fails GREEK-TRANSLIT while *hieros* (G2411, …) passes, and the judge then marks the passing form wrong (R6, john-02). Suggest correcting the G2411 translit to 'hieron' (and scanning for other substantive entries that carry their adjective's translit), or having the checker also accept a translit derived from the Greek lemma itself. Until then, TEMPLATE.md rule 4 could say that when the lexicon's translit disagrees with its Greek lemma, cite the form printed in the verse.
  - **Decision (2026-09-24, the builder):** adopted in substance. The tools now transliterate each lemma's own Greek: `tools/lexicon.py` prints *hieron* for G2411 with a note, and the checker accepts *hieron* and refuses *hieros* for it (LOOP-SPEC.md, SF-10).
- 2026-09-24 · john-02 · TEMPLATE.md rule 4 / rule 'Never invent': the judge marked R6 down for word-study claims attributed to an unnamed 'the lexicon'. Suggest the template say what to call the source: tools/lexicon.py is the STEPBible.org brief lexicon (Tyndale House, Cambridge), whose Greek entries mostly carry an '(AS)' tag for Abbott-Smith, *A Manual Greek Lexicon of the New Testament*, and whose Hebrew entries follow a different, BDB-style outline. A fixed wording (for example 'the STEPBible lexicon') would keep every unit consistent and stop BDAG or Thayer being named for definitions that did not come from them.
  - **Decision (2026-09-24, the builder):** adopted. TEMPLATE.md rule 4 and `tools/lexicon.py` name the STEPBible.org brief lexicon and Abbott-Smith (plan.md D5; LOOP-SPEC.md, SF-11).
- 2026-09-25 · john-00 · TEMPLATE.md Part 5: the overview's Lesson Map is written after some chapter units may already exist (john-02 did). Suggest Part 5 say that the Lesson Map keeps the title, Big Idea, and memory verse of any chapter unit already drafted unless a person says otherwise, and suggest a checker rule comparing each existing study/john-NN.md title and memory verse with its Lesson Map row, so the two cannot drift apart. Also suggest a word on memory-verse length for a mixed group (some natural choices, like John 18:37 and John 21:17, are long).
- 2026-09-25 · john-01 · TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rule 2: the SCRIPTURE-NEARMISS check reads every single-quoted phrase of five or more words, so it also catches text that is deliberately not KJV: a lexicon gloss ('to lead, show the way' matched Exodus 13:21 at 80%) and another translation's rendering under discussion (the rendering of John 1:1 with 'a god' matched the KJV verse at 89%). Suggest rule 2 say that lexicon glosses and other translations' renderings go in *italics* or plain words, never single quotes, so makers don't find this out from a failed run. This matters most in chapters with a well-known rival rendering (John 1:1, John 8:58).
- 2026-09-25 · john-01 · TEMPLATE.md rule 'Practices you can do today' vs the Planner headers and Part 5 table: the Life and Light Planner columns ('What I will do about it this week', 'How I carry it there this week') and the Objectives, Bring Life, and Tier 1 rows all say 'this week', so a maker naturally writes practices scoped to a week, and the judge then marks R7 down because rubric R7 asks that every practice be 'doable within a day' (john-01 iter 1, R7 = 4). Suggest TEMPLATE.md say plainly that a practice may fall within the week but must name one day-sized action (today, tomorrow, or 'pick one day'), and perhaps rename the Planner columns to 'What I will do, and on which day'.
- 2026-09-25 · john-03 · PROMPT.md 'Do one thing' (and perhaps the checker): an interrupted run (here, at Claude's usage limit) can leave a half-written unit, which the next run treats as 'exists'. RUN CONTEXT then said 'iteration 1' with a checker report whose first three items were whole missing sections (Doctrine, Council, Leader's Guide), and the file ended at a `<!-- NEXT -->` marker. PROMPT.md's two cases ('doesn't exist yet' and 'exists and a report lists problems') don't quite fit, and 'fix exactly those problems' could be read as a small patch. Suggest PROMPT.md add a third case: if the file is incomplete (missing sections, or ends in a leftover marker), finish it section by section following TEMPLATE.md, keeping what is written. Also suggest that the checker report a leftover `<!-- NEXT -->`-style marker on its own line, so a partial draft is named as partial.
- 2026-09-25 · john-04 · TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rule 2: five of john-04's six first-run failures were exact KJV phrases in single quotes that failed only because the sentence's own question mark or full stop sat inside the closing quote ('Art thou greater than our father Jacob?' where the KJV continues with a comma). Suggest rule 2 add one line: put your own sentence punctuation outside a single-quoted Scripture phrase, since the near-miss check compares the punctuation too. It would save a checker run on most units.
- 2026-09-25 · john-05 · TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rule 4: a two-word Greek phrase cited with two Strong's numbers in one parenthesis (*huios anthrōpou* (G5207, John 5:27; G444, John 5:27)) fails as STRONGS-LOOSE. Suggest rule 4 say that each Greek word takes its own citation, even inside a phrase (*huios* (G5207, John 5:27) *anthrōpou* (G444, John 5:27)). Also suggest Part 4's Answer Key row say that every Level 4 and Level 5 answer begins 'Model answer, not the only answer:', since the judge marked R8 down on john-04 for one unlabelled Level 5 answer; a fixed wording would make it checkable by script.
- 2026-09-25 · john-06 · TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rules 1–2: rule 1 lets a double-quoted quotation change the case of its first letter and use '...' for a gap, but the near-miss check on single-quoted phrases allows neither: john-06's first run failed on 'He distributed to the disciples, ...' (John 6:11 has 'he' mid-verse) and on two 'except the Father ... draw him' phrases (John 6:44). Suggest rule 2 say plainly that a single-quoted Scripture phrase must match the KJV letter for letter, with no change of case and no '...', or else that the checker give single quotes the same allowances as double quotes. Together with the john-04 note on sentence punctuation, this would likely save a checker run on most units.
- 2026-09-25 · john-07 · run.sh / RUN CONTEXT (the john-03 note again): the half-written unit happened a second time. `reviews/john-07.maker.txt` held only the session-limit message ('You've hit your session limit'), and the unit file ended at `<!-- NEXT -->` after Tier 4, yet RUN CONTEXT called it iteration 1 with no hint that the earlier run was cut off. Besides the john-03 suggestions, run.sh could check the last maker output for a usage-limit message (or a missing STATUS line) and add a RUN CONTEXT line such as 'previous run interrupted: finish the unit', so the next maker doesn't have to piece that together from the checker report.
- 2026-09-25 · john-08 · TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rules 2 and 4: two more easy trips. (a) An article-plus-noun phrase in one italic span before a citation (*tēn archēn* (G746, John 8:25)) fails GREEK-TRANSLIT; rule 4 could say that the italic span before a citation holds exactly one Greek word, the one the Strong's number names. (b) An English gloss of a Greek form in single quotes ('ye shall have lifted up' for *hupsōsēte*) is caught as a near miss of the verse it translates; this is the john-01 note again, and it bites hardest in word studies, so rule 2 could say that glosses of Greek forms go in italics or plain words. Also, PROMPT.md's three-run limit and its advice to write in parts pull against each other: a check on a half-written file only reports missing sections and uses up a run. Suggest PROMPT.md say to run the checker only once the whole skeleton is filled.
- 2026-09-25 · john-09 · TEMPLATE.md Part 1 rules 2 and 3: (a) the near-miss check also catches a *manuscript variant* quoted in single quotes (the critical text's 'We must work the works of him that sent me' at John 9:4 failed as a 95% near miss). Every unit with textual notes will meet this; rule 2 could say that variant readings and other translations' wordings go in italics, alongside the john-01 and john-08 notes on glosses. (b) Single-chapter books need chapter and verse: '3 John 10' fails REF-INVALID, and '3 John 1:10' passes. Rule 3 could say so, since Jude, Philemon, Obadiah, 2 John, and 3 John are cited this way in many commentaries. (c) `tools/greek.py` prints some transliterations with an apostrophe for rough breathing (*he'ōrakas*, *he'autou*, *he'ōs*) and some without the *h* (*ina* for ἵνα); the checker accepted *heōrakas* and *hina*, so it seems to normalise these, but rule 4 or the tool's help text could say which spelling to write.
- 2026-09-25 · john-10 · tools data and tools/lexicon.py (for a person; not a PROMPT/TEMPLATE file): (a) `tools/greek.py "John 10:28"` prints words #1–3 and #6–21 but no #4–5, so *zōēn aiōnion* ('eternal life') cannot be cited at the verse where the chapter's key promise stands; the unit cited *zōēn* at John 10:10 instead. Suggest a scan of the Greek data for verses with gaps in the word numbering. (b) `tools/lexicon.py` cuts long entries with '...' (G2570 *kalos*, G5087, G1097, G3089), so a maker cannot see which sense the lexicon lists a verse under; for *kalos* at John 10:11 the unit had to avoid saying. A `--full` option, or a `--verse "John 10:11"` option that prints the sense line naming that verse, would make these claims checkable. (c) TEMPLATE.md rule 2 could say that a single-quoted Scripture phrase may not contain '...' (the john-06 note again): it is the easiest near miss to write when compressing a verse like Psalm 78:70 or Ezekiel 34:23.
- 2026-09-25 · john-11 · tools data (for a person; not a PROMPT/TEMPLATE file) and PROMPT.md: (a) `tools/greek.py` flags several words whose data-file transliteration ends in a stray 'a' where the Greek has an iota subscript (*doxasthēa* for δοξασθῇ at John 11:4, *peripatēa* at John 11:9–10, *phōnēa* at John 11:43, *autēa* at John 11:25). The tool prints the right form, but a maker unsure whether the checker accepts it will avoid citing the word (john-11 avoided *doxasthē* and *phōnē*). Suggest normalising the data file, or a line in the tool's help saying that the printed form is the one to cite. (b) In this session, shell pipes, `for` loops, and inline Python were refused by the permission mode while plain tool calls joined with `;` ran; `greek.py` on a whole chapter prints about 90 KB. A `--brief` option (content words only: form, Strong's number, parsing code, gloss) would make whole-chapter lookups practical without filters. (c) The fourth SCRIPTURE-NEARMISS in a row from sentence punctuation inside a single-quoted phrase (here a full stop after 'I am the resurrection, and the life' in the Line to Say): the john-04 note again, and worth adopting.
